What does a nilpotent operator determine?

4 October 2026

Can one recover a Hodge structure, or an \(SL_2\)-representation, from a single nilpotent endomorphism \(N\colon M\to M\)? Completing \(N\) to an \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-triple is possible in characteristic zero, but the completion is not canonical. There is nevertheless a canonical construction: \(N\) determines a filtration \(W(N)\), and its associated graded carries a uniquely determined \(SL_2\)-action with lowering operator induced by \(N\). A Hodge structure requires further data; the construction here recovers this graded representation.

We will realize the passage to this graded representation as a degeneration of equivariant vector bundles. The geometry comes from the inclusion \(\mathbb{G}_a\subset SL_2\): the operator defines a representation of the first group, while the special fiber carries a representation of the second. Restriction to the special fiber will describe semisimplification and the loss of extension data.

Except where stated otherwise, \(k\) is a field of characteristic zero and the representations are finite-dimensional and rational.

1. The canonical graded representation

The monodromy filtration centered at zero is the unique finite, exhaustive, separated increasing filtration satisfying

\[ NW_rM\subseteq W_{r-2}M, \qquad N^r\colon\operatorname{gr}^W_rM\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{gr}^W_{-r}M \quad(r\geq0). \]

For a Jordan block \(J_m=k[x]/(x^m)\), with \(N=x\), this is the filtration obtained by giving \(1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}\) weights \(m-1,m-3,\ldots,1-m\). The associated graded is \(\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(k^2)\). For general \(M\), a basis-free formula is

\[ W_rM=\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq0\\a-b=r}} \bigl(\ker N^{a+1}\cap\operatorname{im}N^b\bigr). \]

In particular, every map commuting with the operators preserves these filtrations. On \(\operatorname{gr}^W M\), the grading and the induced lowering operator determine a unique raising operator; its formula is given in section 2.3. Thus the graded \(SL_2\)-representation is canonical and functorial. Transferring it to \(M\) requires an \(N\)-compatible splitting of \(W\). Such splittings exist, but for \(N\neq0\) they cannot be chosen naturally: every \(1+cN\) commutes with \(N\), whereas a grading operator satisfying \([H,N]=-2N\) has \([H,1+cN]=-2cN\).

2. Realizing the filtration as a family

2.1. The punctured plane

To construct the family, first regard \(N\) as a group action.

Since \(N\) is nilpotent, the exponential is a finite sum and satisfies

\[ \exp(aN)\exp(bN)=\exp((a+b)N). \]

Thus \(N\) gives a representation of the additive group. Identify this group with the lower unipotent subgroup of \(G=SL_2\):

\[ U=\left\{u(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\a&1\end{pmatrix}\right\}, \qquad u(a)\cdot m=\exp(aN)m. \]

A \(U\)-representation determines a \(G\)-equivariant vector bundle on \(G/U\), with that representation as its fiber at the identity coset. This turns the comparison of \(U\)- and \(G\)-representations into a question about equivariant bundles.

The group \(SL_2\) acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of its standard plane, and a determinant-one matrix fixes \(e_2=(0,1)\) exactly when it belongs to \(U\). Consequently,

\[ G/U\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{A}^2\setminus\{0\}, \qquad gU\longmapsto ge_2. \]

The punctured plane parametrizes the cosets; \(M\) is the fiber and can have any dimension. The associated bundle is \(G\times^U M\), with projection \([g,m]\mapsto ge_2\) and relation \([gu,m]=[g,um]\). Its fiber at \(e_2\) is the original \((M,N)\).

If the \(U\)-representation extends to a \(G\)-representation \(Q\), this bundle has the constant presentation \(\mathcal{O}\otimes Q\), with \(G\) acting on both the base and \(Q\). For a chosen identification \(M\simeq Q|_U\), the presentation sends \([g,m]\) to \((ge_2,gm)\). Choosing this presentation amounts to choosing an \(SL_2\)-completion. The canonical family will instead arise by extending the bundle towards the origin.

2.2. The blow-up and the Rees construction

To see a filtration in this geometry, consider approaching the origin along a line. A nonzero vector has stabilizer \(U\), which records the operator. A line has a larger stabilizer: besides \(U\), it contains a torus that can record weights. For the line \(ke_2\), this stabilizer is

\[ B=T\ltimes U,\qquad T=\{\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\},\qquad tu(a)t^{-1}=u(t^{-2}a). \]

We therefore keep the line when the vector reaches zero. The space of pairs \((v,\ell)\), with \(v\in\ell\subset k^2\), does precisely this: it is the blow-up of the origin. Away from zero, \(\ell=kv\) is forced; over zero, the possible lines form \(\mathbb{P}^1\). Thus

\[ \operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb{A}^2 =\{(v,\ell)\in\mathbb{A}^2\times\mathbb{P}^1:v\in\ell\} \simeq\operatorname{Tot}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1). \]

The exceptional point \((0,ke_2)\) has stabilizer \(B\). The limiting fiber can therefore carry both a unipotent action and the torus grading needed to record weights.

Write \(\mathscr{X}=[\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb{A}^2/G]\). Here a bundle on \([Z/G]\) means a \(G\)-equivariant bundle on \(Z\), and a bundle on \(BH=[\operatorname{Spec}k/H]\) means an \(H\)-representation. The stabilizers just computed give the diagram

\[ \begin{array}{ccccc} BB&\xrightarrow{\ i\ }&\mathscr{X}&\xleftarrow{\ j\ }&BU\\ {\scriptstyle p}\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\Vert\\ BG&\hookrightarrow&[\mathbb{A}^2/G]&\hookleftarrow&BU. \end{array} \]

The bottom row uses the origin, whose stabilizer is all of \(G\); the top row uses the exceptional directions, whose stabilizer is \(B\). The map \(p\colon BB\to BG\) forgets the direction, so \(p^*\) is restriction of a representation from \(G\) to \(B\).

It suffices to work along the direction \(ke_2\). Write \(L=ke_2\), with coordinate \(z\), and use the slice \(z\mapsto(ze_2,ke_2)\). Its translates give

\[ G\times^B L\simeq\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb{A}^2, \qquad \mathscr{X}\simeq[L/B]. \]

The torus acts on the slice by \(z\mapsto t^{-1}z\), while \(U\) acts trivially. We have reduced the construction to a family on a line: at \(z=1\) its fiber is a \(U\)-representation, and at \(z=0\) it is a \(B\)-representation.

A Rees calculation

For example, take \(M=ku\oplus kv\), with \(Nv=u\) and \(Nu=0\). Over \(z\neq0\), transport the fiber at \(1\) using \(\tau_z=\operatorname{diag}(z^{-1},z)\). Since

\[ \tau_z e_2=ze_2,\qquad \tau_z^{-1}u(a)\tau_z=u(z^{-2}a), \]

the transported operator is \(z^{-2}N\). In the fixed basis \(u,v\), it sends \(v\) to \(z^{-2}u\), which has a pole. Declare instead that the regular basis of the extending bundle is

\[ e_u=z^{-1}u,\qquad e_v=zv. \]

Then \(z^{-2}N(e_v)=e_u\), so the operator extends regularly to zero. The negative power in \(e_u\) describes this basis relative to the old trivialization on the punctured line; the new bundle has regular sections \(k[z]e_u\oplus k[z]e_v\). At \(z=1\) we get \(u,v\); at \(z=0\), their new basis classes have weights \(-1,1\) and give the two pieces \(\ker N\) and \(M/\ker N\) of the associated graded.

Changing the old lift to \(v+cu\) changes its regular section to

\[ z(v+cu)=e_v+cz^2e_u. \]

It changes neither the module of regular sections nor the special-fiber classes.

Compatible filtrations

For a general \((M,N)\), the same transport gives \(z^{-2}N\). We must choose which Laurent-polynomial sections are to be regular at zero. A filtration records exactly these powers of \(z\).

For a finite, exhaustive, separated increasing filtration \(W\) on \(M\), take

\[ \mathcal{R}_W(M)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}W_rM\,z^r \subset M[z,z^{-1}]. \]

The coordinate function \(z\) has \(T\)-weight \(+1\); give \(M\) weight zero in the Laurent trivialization, so \(mz^r\) has weight \(r\). An adapted basis with filtration indices \(r_1,\ldots,r_d\) gives a \(k[z]\)-basis \(z^{r_1}v_1,\ldots,z^{r_d}v_d\), proving that \(\mathcal{R}_W(M)\) defines a vector bundle. The operator

\[ \widetilde{N}(mz^r)=(Nm)z^{r-2} \]

preserves this lattice exactly when \(NW_rM\subseteq W_{r-2}M\). Under this condition, \(\exp(a\widetilde{N})\) gives a regular \(U\)-action; together with the grading it makes the Rees bundle \(B\)-equivariant.

Conversely, the regular sections of any such extension form a graded lattice in \(M[z,z^{-1}]\). Recover its filtration by \(W_rM=\{m:mz^r\text{ is a regular section}\}\). Regularity of \(z^{-2}N\) gives precisely the same compatibility condition. Thus extending the bundle from \(BU\) to \(\mathscr{X}\) is equivalent to choosing a compatible filtration, and bundle morphisms correspond to filtration-preserving maps commuting with \(N\).

The fibers of this family explain what the extension remembers:

\[ \begin{aligned} z=1:\quad &\mathcal{R}_W(M)/(z-1)\simeq M, &&\widetilde{N}|_1=N,\\ z=0:\quad &\mathcal{R}_W(M)/z\mathcal{R}_W(M) \simeq\bigoplus_r W_rM/W_{r-1}M, &&\widetilde{N}|_0=\operatorname{gr}^W N. \end{aligned} \]

At \(z=1\) we recover the original nilpotent operator. At \(z=0\) we obtain its associated graded, with a torus weight \(r\) on \(W_rM/W_{r-1}M\) and an operator of degree \(-2\). The family is canonical once the filtration is specified; identifying its two fibers requires a choice of splitting.

2.3. The boundary condition selects the monodromy filtration

A compatible filtration gives a \(B\)-representation on the boundary, but we want the full Lefschetz \(SL_2\)-symmetry. Although the bundle on the blow-up is \(G\)-equivariant, a fixed boundary fiber is acted on only by its stabilizer \(B\): elements of \(G\) outside \(B\) move the boundary point. The additional condition is that this \(B\)-representation be the restriction of a \(G\)-representation. In terms of the map \(p\colon BB\to BG\) above, we require

\[ i^*\mathcal{E}\simeq p^*Q \qquad\text{for some }Q\in\operatorname{Vect}(BG) =\operatorname{Rep}(G). \]

Equivalently, the bundle on the exceptional divisor \(\mathbb{P}^1\) must be \(G\)-equivariantly isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\otimes Q\), where \(G\) acts diagonally on the base and on \(Q\). The condition concerns the boundary fiber; it does not choose an \(SL_2\)-action on \(M\).

A \(B\)-representation is a graded space \(V = \bigoplus_r V_r\) with \(N \colon V_r \to V_{r-2}\). It extends to \(SL_2\) exactly when

\[ N^r \colon V_r \xrightarrow{\sim} V_{-r} \qquad (r \geq 0). \]

Indeed, this holds on every irreducible \(SL_2\)-representation. Conversely, these isomorphisms give the primitive decomposition

\[ P_r = \ker(N^{r+1} \colon V_r \to V_{-r-2}), \qquad V = \bigoplus_{r \geq 0}\ \bigoplus_{a=0}^{r} N^aP_r. \]

The unique raising operator is

\[ E(N^ap) = a(r-a+1)N^{a-1}p \qquad (p \in P_r), \]

with \(Ep = 0\). Together with \(H|_{V_r} = r\), it gives the standard \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-action on each string, hence an algebraic \(SL_2\)-action over \(k\).

Applied to the boundary of a Rees bundle, this criterion says that its filtration must satisfy

\[ N^r\colon\operatorname{gr}^W_rM \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{gr}^W_{-r}M \qquad(r\geq0). \]

Together with \(NW_rM\subseteq W_{r-2}M\), these are exactly the defining conditions for the monodromy filtration centered at zero. Consequently, among all extensions of \((M,N)\) across the exceptional divisor, the boundary condition selects precisely \(\mathcal{R}_{W(N)}(M)\).

Let \(\operatorname{Vect}^{\mathrm{Lef}}(\mathscr{X})\) be the full subcategory of vector bundles whose restriction to \(BB\) is the restriction of an \(SL_2\)-representation.

Proposition. Restriction induces an equivalence of \(k\)-linear categories

\[ j^* \colon \operatorname{Vect}^{\mathrm{Lef}}(\mathscr{X}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Rep}(U). \]

A quasi-inverse is \((M,N) \mapsto \mathcal{R}_{W(N)}(M)\).

Proof. By the Rees description, a bundle extending \((M,N)\) amounts to a compatible filtration \(W\). The boundary condition is precisely the Lefschetz condition on \(\operatorname{gr}^W M\), which characterizes \(W(N)\). Every map commuting with the nilpotent operators preserves their monodromy filtrations and therefore extends to a Rees morphism. The extension is unique because a map between vector bundles on \(L\) is determined on \(L^{\times}\). This proves essential surjectivity and full faithfulness. \(\square\)

Restriction \(j^*\) preserves short exact sequences of vector bundles, but its quasi-inverse need not send a short exact sequence of representations to a short exact sequence of vector bundles. Section 4 computes this failure of exactness.

3. What is lost at the special fiber?

The special fiber retains the Jordan block lengths but kills certain morphisms. This gives a geometric description of semisimplification.

3.1. Semisimplification and tensor products

Restriction of the canonical extension to the boundary defines a functor

\[ \mathsf{S}\colon\operatorname{Rep}(U)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Rep}(SL_2), \qquad (M,N)\longmapsto\operatorname{gr}^{W(N)}M. \]

It is symmetric monoidal: tensoring two extensions preserves the boundary condition, so uniqueness identifies their tensor product with the canonical extension of the tensor product. In filtration language,

\[ W_r(M\otimes M')=\sum_{a+b=r}W_aM\otimes W_bM', \qquad N_{M\otimes M'}=N\otimes1+1\otimes N'. \]

We can see exactly what \(\mathsf{S}\) forgets by using Jordan blocks. It sends \(J_m\) to \(\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(k^2)\), kills every map between blocks of different sizes, and sends

\[ a_0+a_1N+\cdots+a_{m-1}N^{m-1} \in\operatorname{End}_U(J_m) \quad\longmapsto\quad a_0\operatorname{id}. \]

These calculations show that \(\mathsf{S}\) is full and essentially surjective. Its kernel is the ideal of negligible morphisms: those \(f\colon M\to M'\) for which \(\operatorname{tr}(gf)=0\) for every \(g\colon M'\to M\). Indeed, between unequal indecomposable blocks every such composition is nilpotent; on \(J_m\), the trace pairing detects exactly the constant coefficient, since \(m\neq0\) in \(k\). Applying this blockwise gives

\[ \operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{G}_a)/\{\text{negligible morphisms}\} \simeq\operatorname{Rep}(SL_2). \]

Thus the exceptional boundary realizes the semisimplification of \(\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{G}_a)\). The functor is not exact: it sends both maps in \(0\to J_1\to J_2\to J_1\to0\) to zero because their source and target weights differ. Likewise, \(\mathsf{S}(N)=0\) when \(N\) is viewed as a filtration-preserving endomorphism; the lowering operator on the boundary is its degree \(-2\) component instead.

A concrete payoff is the Jordan form of a sum of commuting nilpotents on a tensor product. Clebsch--Gordan gives

\[ (J_m\otimes J_n,\ N\otimes1+1\otimes N) \simeq\bigoplus_{a=0}^{\min(m,n)-1}J_{m+n-1-2a}. \]

For example, \(J_2\otimes J_3\simeq J_4\oplus J_2\). The block lengths are determined without choosing a splitting of either monodromy filtration; the displayed direct-sum decomposition itself need not be canonical. The semisimplification result is classical; the point here is its realization by restriction to the exceptional boundary.

3.2. Vanishing along the exceptional divisor

We can measure what the boundary functor forgets. Let \(E\) be the exceptional divisor and \(\mathcal{E}_M\) the canonical extension of \(M\). For \(q\geq0\), the extension of an intertwiner \(f\colon M\to M'\) factors through \(\mathcal{E}_{M'}(-qE)\) exactly when

\[ f(W_rM)\subseteq W_{r-q}M'\qquad\text{for every }r. \]

Indeed, on the slice, factoring through \(-qE\) means divisibility by \(z^q\) in the Rees lattice. The case \(q=1\) says that negligible morphisms are precisely the maps vanishing along \(E\). The lower bounds on vanishing order add under composition.

For \(J_m\to J_n\), the usual basis consists of the maps sending \(1\) to \(x^b\), with \(\max(n-m,0)\leq b\leq n-1\). The source generator has weight \(m-1\), and its image has weight \(n-1-2b\). Their difference gives the vanishing orders

\[ |m-n|,\ |m-n|+2,\ \ldots,\ m+n-2. \]

In particular, \(N^a\in\operatorname{End}(J_m)\) vanishes to order \(2a\) for \(0\leq a<m\). For \(m>1\), the identity on the Rees slice is

\[ \mathcal{R}(N)=z^2\widetilde{N}. \]

Thus \(\mathsf{S}(N)=0\) is compatible with a nonzero lowering operator at the boundary: the latter is the leading term after removing the two powers of the normal parameter.

4. Why extensions behave differently

There are two related issues to separate: extending an individual bundle across the origin, and extending a short exact sequence as a short exact sequence. The first always works in this surface geometry; the second need not.

4.1. Extending bundles across the origin

The bundle on the punctured plane also extends uniquely across the origin itself. Unlike the Jordan-chain proof, the following argument works over any field. Let \(\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to[\mathbb{A}^2/G]\) be the blow-down. Over any field, there are equivalences

\[ \operatorname{Vect}([\mathbb{A}^2/G]) \xrightarrow[\sim]{\ \pi^*\ } \operatorname{Vect}^{\mathrm{Lef}}(\mathscr{X}) \xrightarrow[\sim]{\ j^*\ } \operatorname{Rep}(U). \]

Here the superscript \(\mathrm{Lef}\) still means that the boundary \(B\)-representation comes from \(G\); it is a condition on the group action, not just its differential. Both displayed functors preserve short exact sequences of vector bundles; the inverse extension functor from \(\operatorname{Rep}(U)\) need not do so.

The proof uses two facts about this surface. First, a vector bundle on \(\mathbb{A}^2\setminus\{0\}\) has a unique reflexive extension across the origin, and a reflexive coherent sheaf on a regular surface is locally free. Morphisms extend uniquely as well. The \(G\)-equivariant structure extends by applying the same uniqueness to its action isomorphism on \(G\times\mathbb{A}^2\); the cocycle identity extends on \(G\times G\times\mathbb{A}^2\). Consequently, restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Vect}([\mathbb{A}^2/G])\) with \(\operatorname{Rep}(U)\), without a characteristic assumption.

Second, a vector bundle on \(\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb{A}^2\) descends to \(\mathbb{A}^2\) precisely when it is trivial on the exceptional curve. Here is a way to see the nontrivial direction. Write \(E\simeq\mathbb{P}^1\) for that curve and \(I=\mathcal{O}(-E)\) for its ideal. If \(\mathcal{F}|_E\simeq\mathcal{O}_E^d\), then

\[ I^n/I^{n+1}\simeq\mathcal{O}_E(n), \qquad H^1(E,\mathcal{O}_E(n))=0\quad(n\geq0). \]

A basis on \(E\) therefore lifts successively to compatible bases on all its infinitesimal thickenings: the obstruction to lifting sections lies in the displayed \(H^1\), and a lifted basis remains a basis by Nakayama. Formal functions, together with \(\pi_*\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}\), now gives

\[ (\pi_*\mathcal{F})^{\wedge}_0 \simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{A}^2,0}^{\,d}. \]

Faithful flatness of completion shows that \(\pi_*\mathcal{F}\) is locally free near the origin. The lifted bases identify the evaluation map on \(E\); hence \(\pi^*\pi_*\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\) is an isomorphism there and on its complement, so everywhere. Equivariance descends by the uniqueness of extension just used.

Finally, for a \(G\)-equivariant bundle on \(E=G/B\), underlying triviality is exactly our boundary condition. If it is trivial, take \(Q=H^0(E,\mathcal{F}|_E)\); the evaluation map \(\mathcal{O}_E\otimes Q\to\mathcal{F}|_E\) is a \(G\)-equivariant isomorphism. This proves the claimed equivalences. In characteristic zero, the pullback of the reflexive extension must therefore be the monodromy Rees bundle already constructed.

4.2. Filtered complexes and the Rees defect

To keep track of a sequence that stops being exact, we pass from vector bundles to complexes. In this setting the associated graded is formed by a cofiber, so it retains the derived information at the special fiber.

Write \(D(k)\) for the stable \(\infty\)-category of complexes of \(k\)-vector spaces. With our increasing-filtration convention, a filtered complex is an object of \(\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z},D(k))\), with transition maps \(F_{r-1}\to F_r\). Its underlying and graded objects are

\[ F_{\infty}=\operatorname*{colim}_r F_r, \qquad \operatorname{gr}_r F=\operatorname{cofib}(F_{r-1}\to F_r). \]

The Rees construction identifies this category, with its convolution tensor product, with \(D_{\mathrm{qc}}([\mathbb{A}^1/\mathbb{G}_m])\). For the corresponding graded \(k[z]\)-complex \(\mathcal{R}(F)\), with \(z\) in degree \(1\), the two restrictions are

\[ \mathcal{R}(F)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{k[z]}k_{z=1}\simeq F_{\infty}, \qquad \mathcal{R}(F)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{k[z]}k_{z=0} \simeq\bigoplus_r\operatorname{gr}_r F. \]

At zero the grading is retained. For the finite filtrations of sections 1-2, the Rees module is free over \(k[z]\), so these derived fibers are the ordinary fibers already computed. The extra operator \(\widetilde{N}\) supplies the \(U\)-action needed to pass from this filtered model to the blow-up quotient.

Both restriction functors above are exact in the stable sense. This does not make the assignment \((M,N)\mapsto W(N)\) exact. For the sequence \(J_1\to J_2\to J_1\), the Rees maps in the basis \(z^{-1}u,zv\) from section 2.2 are

\[ k[z]\longrightarrow k[z]^2\longrightarrow k[z], \qquad 1\longmapsto(z,0),\qquad(a,b)\longmapsto zb. \]

Forgetting the grading, this complex has \(k[z]/(z)\) as its cohomology at both the middle and the last term. It becomes exact after inverting \(z\), but it was never an exact triangle of filtered objects.

4.3. The Koszul complex of the origin

The same loss of exactness can be seen directly on the plane. The tautological vector at a point of \(k^2\) gives a complex that is exact away from the origin and has a cokernel at the origin. For the standard representation \(S=k^2\), the stack \([S/G]\) is the total space of the rank-two bundle on \(BG\) associated with \(S\). Write \(i_0\colon BG\hookrightarrow[S/G]\) for its zero section. The tautological vector \(s\) gives its Koszul resolution, with terms in degrees \(-2,-1,0\):

\[ i_{0*}\mathcal{O}_{BG} \simeq [\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }S\otimes\mathcal{O} \xrightarrow{\ s\wedge-\ }\mathcal{O}]. \]

Here the determinant form identifies \(\bigwedge^2S\) with \(k\). On the punctured plane, \(s\) never vanishes, so this complex is exact. Its fiber at \(e_2\) is the \(U\)-equivariant sequence

\[ 0 \longrightarrow k \xrightarrow{\ 1\mapsto e_2\ } S \xrightarrow{\ e_2\wedge-\ } k \longrightarrow 0. \]

It is nonsplit: \(U\) fixes \(e_2\) and sends \(e_1\) to \(e_1+ae_2\), so the invariant line \(ke_2\) has no invariant complement. Thus the geometry of the missing origin produces the simplest nontrivial extension of nilpotent representations. In particular, \(\operatorname{Rep}(U)\) cannot be equivalent to the semisimple category \(\operatorname{Rep}(SL_2)\). The equivalence in section 2.3 uses bundles on the blow-up with a boundary condition.

Tensoring with \(\operatorname{Sym}^n S\) and using Clebsch–Gordan gives

\[ 0 \longrightarrow J_{n+1} \longrightarrow J_{n+2} \oplus J_n \longrightarrow J_{n+1} \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (n \geq 0), \]

where \(J_0=0\). One explicit sequence with these terms is obtained from the maps

\[ J_m \longrightarrow J_{m+1} \oplus J_{m-1}, \qquad v \longmapsto (xv,\overline{v}), \]
\[ J_{m+1} \oplus J_{m-1} \longrightarrow J_m, \qquad (a,b) \longmapsto \overline{a}-xb. \]

Their exactness can be checked directly. In the Grothendieck group defined using short exact sequences of \(U\)-representations, this gives \(2[J_m] = [J_{m+1}] + [J_{m-1}]\). In fact every \(J_m\) has a filtration with \(m\) trivial quotients, so \(K_0(\operatorname{Rep}(U)) \simeq \mathbb{Z}\) via dimension. This numerical statement does not retain the extension data or the Lefschetz boundary condition.

The same Koszul resolution gives a second calculation at the closed stratum. For a \(G\)-representation \(Q\), pulling the resolution of \(i_{0*}Q\) back to the zero section makes its differentials vanish, hence

\[ Li_0^*i_{0*}Q \simeq Q\oplus(Q\otimes S)[1]\oplus Q[2]. \]

Its class is \((2-[S])[Q]\) in \(K_0(BG)\). The exact sequence on \(BU\) and this derived self-intersection on \(BG\) are two restrictions of the same Koszul complex.

4.4. The derived categories differ

Put \(Y=[\mathbb{A}^2/SL_2]\). The regular-surface argument in section 4.1 shows that restriction \(\operatorname{Vect}(Y)\to\operatorname{Vect}(BU)\) is an equivalence of ordinary categories. In characteristic zero, extensions of \(U\)-representations can be computed from

\[ R\Gamma(BU,M)\simeq[M\xrightarrow{\ N\ }M] \qquad\text{in cohomological degrees }0,1. \]

For two representations, apply this to \(\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,M')\), where the differential sends \(f\) to \(N'f-fN\). Thus ordinary intertwiners and their first extensions are the kernel and cokernel of the same operator.

Taking \(M=k\) gives the contrast

\[ \operatorname{Ext}^1_Y(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})=0, \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^1_{BU}(k,k)=k. \]

The first equality follows because \(\mathbb{A}^2\) is affine and invariants under \(SL_2\) are exact; the second follows from \([k\xrightarrow{0}k]\). Consequently, \(\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\to\operatorname{Perf}(BU)\) is not fully faithful. The nonsplit sequence \(0\to k\to k^2\to k\to0\) extends across the origin as the tautological Koszul complex, whose cokernel there is \(i_{0*}\mathcal{O}_{BG}\). It therefore fails to extend as a short exact sequence of vector bundles. The missing origin is invisible to individual bundles and their ordinary morphisms, but remains visible to extensions and perfect complexes.

5. What changes in positive characteristic?

The extension theorem in section 4.1 still gives a canonical bundle and boundary representation over a field of characteristic \(p\). What changes is the input: a nilpotent differential no longer determines an action of \(\mathbb{G}_a\). We must keep the full group action. The following example will show that its higher powers of the group parameter can change the canonical weights.

Over a field of characteristic \(p>0\), a \(U\)-representation must instead be described by its full coaction

\[ \delta(m) = \sum_{n \geq 0} D_n(m) \otimes a^n, \qquad D_0=1, \qquad D_rD_s = \binom{r+s}{r}D_{r+s}, \]

with only finitely many nonzero terms. The same Rees construction works with \(D_nW_r \subseteq W_{r-2n}\), using \(\widetilde{D}_n(mz^r) = D_n(m)z^{r-2n}\).

For example, \(a \mapsto \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\a^p&1\end{pmatrix}\) is nontrivial although its differential is zero. It is the restriction of the Frobenius twist of the standard \(SL_2\)-representation, whose weights are \(p\) and \(-p\). The classical condition involving powers of \(N=D_1\) therefore no longer characterizes extension to \(SL_2\). Replacing \(N^r\) by \(D_r\) does not suffice either: in characteristic three, \(\operatorname{Sym}^4(k^2)\) has

\[ D_2(e_1^3e_2) = \binom{3}{2}e_1e_2^3 = 0, \]

although its weights \(2\) and \(-2\) are both one-dimensional.

The extension theorem still supplies a unique compatible filtration satisfying the geometric boundary condition, with compatibility expressed using all the \(D_n\). The existence and uniqueness come from the surface geometry; an explicit description of this filtration in general would still be useful. This extension theorem is over a field; no corresponding assertion over an arbitrary base is made here.

A mixed Frobenius example

Take \(M=\langle u,v\rangle\) in characteristic \(p>0\), with

\[ \rho(a)u=u,\qquad \rho(a)v=v+(a+a^p)u. \]

The geometric filtration has \(u\) in weight \(-p\) and \(v\) in weight \(p\). In the Rees basis \(e_u=z^{-p}u\), \(e_v=z^pv\), the action is

\[ \rho_z(a)e_u=e_u,\qquad \rho_z(a)e_v=e_v+(az^{2p-2}+a^p)e_u. \]

At \(z=1\) we recover the original representation; at \(z=0\) only the Frobenius term remains. The boundary is the Frobenius twist of the standard \(SL_2\)-representation, so the preceding uniqueness result identifies this as the canonical filtration.

The original representation does not even extend to \(B\) on the same two-dimensional space. Indeed, its unique \(U\)-fixed line is \(ku\). If a torus action existed, choose weight vectors \(u,v\) of weights \(\alpha,\beta\). The relation between \(T\) and \(U\) would force

\[ t^{\alpha-\beta}(a+a^p) =t^{-2}a+t^{-2p}a^p. \]

Comparing coefficients requires both \(\alpha-\beta=-2\) and \(\alpha-\beta=-2p\), which are distinct characters of the algebraic torus in every characteristic. Thus this example gives a \(G\)-equivariant bundle on \(\mathbb{A}^2\) that is not equivariantly a constant representation bundle, while its canonical boundary still carries a full \(G\)-representation. It also shows why the differential \(D_1\) cannot recover the geometric filtration: its ordinary monodromy weights would have been \(-1,1\).

6. Comparison with prismatic gauges

Bhatt's lectures on prismatic gauges give a mixed-characteristic version of the surface-purity argument in section 4: vector bundles extend across a missing point, but the resulting equivalence does not preserve all derived extension data. For a perfect field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), put \(A=W(k)\). The gauge base is

\[ k^{\mathrm{Nyg}} =\bigl[\operatorname{Spf}(A[u,t]/(ut-p)) / \mathbb{G}_m\bigr], \qquad \deg u=1,\quad\deg t=-1, \]

where \(\operatorname{Spf}\) denotes \(p\)-adic completion. A gauge is represented by a derived \(p\)-complete graded module: its components have maps \(u\colon M_i\to M_{i+1}\), \(t\colon M_{i+1}\to M_i\), with both composites equal to multiplication by \(p\).

To see the surface analogy, work first with the algebraic quotient before completion. The opens \(u\neq0\) and \(t\neq0\) each have quotient \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), and their intersection has quotient \(\operatorname{Spec}A[1/p]\). Together they omit only the closed point \(u=t=0\) on the regular surface \(\operatorname{Spec}(A[u,t]/(ut-p))\). The purity argument therefore describes vector bundles by two finite free \(A\)-lattices \(M_u,M_t\), with an identification \(M_u[1/p]\simeq M_t[1/p]\); formal GAGA gives the same description on the gauge base. In these coordinates, a vector bundle \(F\)-gauge additionally specifies an \(A\)-linear isomorphism \(\varphi^*M_u\simeq M_t\), where \(\varphi\colon A\to A\) is the Witt-vector Frobenius. This is extra integral data, distinct from the identification after inverting \(p\).

As in section 4.4, this ordinary equivalence does not give an equivalence of perfect-complex categories. Here is its simplest derived defect: the endomorphism complex of the trivial pair in the gluing category is

\[ \operatorname{fib}\bigl(A\oplus A \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a-b}A[1/p]\bigr). \]

Its \(H^0\) is \(A\), but its \(H^1\) is \(A[1/p]/A\); the original structure sheaf has no such \(H^1\). The \(a^p\) term in our representation example does not supply this Frobenius gluing.
